Decision skills

1. Separate facts from assumptions.
Write down what you know, then identify what you are guessing.
2. Compare alternatives.
A decision is stronger when you consider at least two realistic alternatives.
3. Consider the downside.
Ask: “What is the worst realistic outcome, and can I handle it?”
4. Avoid rushing.
Important decisions deserve enough time to avoid emotion-driven choices.
5. Review your decisions.
After some time, ask what happened. The goal is not perfect predictions—it is better thinking.

A simple rule

For small, reversible decisions: decide quickly. For important, difficult-to-reverse decisions: slow down, gather evidence, compare options and check your assumptions.
